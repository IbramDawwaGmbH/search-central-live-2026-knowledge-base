## v2.13.0 — 2026-10-08
**The web edition has wider pages, it is Deep Dive only (the style switch is gone), and its water now moves, gently. No claim, thing or kit item changed.**

- **Wider pages.** The page frame grows from 76rem to 92rem (about 1,470 px on a large screen), the single-column pages from 52rem to 68rem and the side column from 17.5rem to 19rem, and the main column no longer stops at 48rem. A claim's text now runs up to 84 characters a line (was 70), requirements and facts up to 72 (were 60 and 62), and introductions and prose up to 76 (were 66 and 68). The widths are four values at the top of `_system/site/site.css` (`--wrap-max`, `--wrap-narrow`, `--side-w` and `--measure`), and the reef art and the diver bot in the page heads and the footer move out with the frame.
- **No style switch.** The web edition, the mindmap and the Reef map show Deep Dive only: light water, or night water when the computer is set to dark. The style switch is gone from the masthead, the phone menu and the headers of both maps, and the browser no longer keeps a style choice: a reader who had picked Art Deco gets Deep Dive, and the old choice is cleared. The tab icon is the Diver bot's head. Art Deco stays available for a whole rebuild: set `default: art-deco` in `_system/theme.yaml` and rebuild, and every page, the mindmap and the Reef map (then called the Graph again) are in Art Deco, with the matching tab icon.
- **Gentle motion.** The Deep Dive pages come alive, quietly and only as decoration, all of it behind the text:
  - **Sunlight.** Soft light shafts sway slowly through the top of every page head (the home page's scene already has its own rays). They stay in the top of the head and fade out downward, so nothing moves behind the text you read.
  - **The reef fish swim.** The yellow fish, the red fish and the little school in a page head swim on the spot, the yellow and red ones wag their tails, and their bubble trail rises (on a screen narrower than about 1160 px, where the reef reaches under the lead, the yellow and red fish and the trail keep still). Now and then a small, faint school crosses the top of the head from right to left.
  - **Clickable fish.** Click or tap a fish in a page head and it darts off the way it faces, then swims back into place a few seconds later. Text and links always stay on top: a click on them never moves a fish.
  - **Night plankton.** In night water (dark mode) tiny specks drift up and twinkle in the page heads, the home page's scene and the footer. Light water has none.
  - **The surface rolls.** The wave under the masthead rolls slowly sideways.
  - **The seabed sways.** The ferns and fan corals in the footer sway a few degrees about their roots.
  - **The Diver bot bobs and blinks.** In the page heads, the footer, the home page and the empty states the bot floats gently and blinks every few seconds, each bot at its own pace. An empty state's bot tilts its head once with a small question bubble, and so does the search bot when nothing matches. When a search finds results, the search bot now stays above them ("Here is what I found on the reef") and blows one bubble, instead of disappearing, so the results no longer jump up the page.
  - **The home figures count up** from 0 the first time they come into view, in about 1.3 seconds, and end as exactly the original numbers (3/3 counts only the 3). Screen readers get the real number all the time.
  - **Smooth page changes.** Moving from page to page cross-fades quickly while the masthead stays still, also to and from the mindmap and the Reef map. The back and forward buttons work as before, on a web server and from the folder (`file://`).
  - **It switches off.** With "reduce motion" set on the computer, nothing moves and the pages look exactly as before, picture for picture: the fish, the bot and the seabed are the still pictures, and the rays, plankton, crossing school and bubbles are not shown. A click on a fish then does nothing. The page's own animations pause while they are off screen or the tab is hidden (the blink, the swaying seabed and the tail wags run inside their pictures and keep going; they cost very little). Art Deco stays still.
  - **Under the hood.** Only movement and fading are animated, so pages stay smooth and nothing shifts the layout; no page scrolls sideways from 320 px to 1680 px. The moving pictures are new files next to the still ones in `assets/ocean/` (the reef as four layers that stack into the old accent picture, the crossing school, the moving seabed, the blinking bots, the question bubble), drawn by new options in `_system/ocean_art.py` that are off by default, so the PDFs and the README art are unchanged. Two new tests, `test_deep_dive_motion_files_markup_and_reduced_motion` and `test_deep_dive_motion_runs`, check the files, the markup, a still page with reduced motion, and the motion, the count-up, a fish click and the widths with it on (70 tests in all).
- **Unchanged.** The claims, the 90 things and their mentions (the precision review of the sample still stands at 0 wrong in 100), the kits, the agent pack apart from its version, the Reef map's data and layout, the Art Deco look itself, and all PDFs and brand files (byte-identical to v2.12.0). Two rebuilds give identical files.

## v2.12.0 — 2026-10-05
**The Reef map now moves under your hand. Drag the water to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, drag bubbles out of the way, grow a neighbour's own neighbours where it sits, and right-click for more. A plain click still puts a bubble in the centre, the back button still returns, and the map still opens in the same neat layout every time. No claim, thing or kit item changed.**

- **Pan and zoom.** Drag the background to move the map. The mouse wheel or a trackpad zooms around the pointer, from 15% to 400%. Double-click empty water to fit the map to its box. The − / Fit / + buttons and the zoom label follow the wheel, and Fit gives the same view the map opened with.
- **A preview on hover.** Pointing at a bubble shows a small card next to the pointer, kept on screen: its name, kind, counts and one-line summary, and for a claim its label and grade. Moving the keyboard focus to a bubble shows the same card; Esc hides it.
- **Drag bubbles.** Drag a bubble to move it. Its neighbours ease aside, and the links follow. Letting go never recentres the map, so only a plain click does. **Reset layout** puts every bubble back.
- **Grow in place.** The small **+** on a bubble, the <kbd>+</kbd> key or the menu grows that bubble's neighbours out of it without moving the centre, eight at a time with a "+8 more (of 45)" bubble, so you can follow a path several steps out. A grown bubble can grow again. **−** (or the <kbd>−</kbd> key) collapses it.
- **Right-click menu.** Right-click a bubble, long-press it on a phone, or press the menu key or Shift+F10 on the focused bubble: open its card, open its topic page, centre here, expand or collapse, pin, or hide. A pinned bubble stays on the map when you move to another centre. The arrow keys, Home, End and Enter work in the menu, and Esc closes it. Right-clicking empty water offers Fit, Reset layout, Collapse everything grown, Show the hidden nodes and Unpin all.
- **Trail.** A row of the centres you visited, across the top of the map. Click one to go back to it. It follows the browser's back and forward buttons and stays after a reload until you press **Clear**.
- **Living layout, optional.** Off by default. Switched on, the bubbles drift into place and make room when you drag one, then come to rest within a few seconds; they bob gently for about half a minute after they last moved and then stop, so the page does no work while you read. It stays off when your computer asks for reduced motion, and switching it off puts back the neat layout.
- **On a phone or tablet.** One finger pans, two fingers pinch to zoom, a tap shows the preview with buttons (Centre, Expand, Collapse, More…), a double tap centres, and a long press opens the menu. After growing a bubble, the map pans to show the new bubbles and zooms out only as far as it must, never below 66%, so the labels stay readable.
- **A hint you can close.** One line under the map says what to do ("Drag to move · scroll to zoom · right-click for more", or the touch wording on a phone). Closing it is remembered.
- **Unchanged.** Click to recentre, the address (`graph.html#ent:googlebot`) and the back button, the filters, hop depth, search, the side panel (still equal to the card for every one of the 90 things), keyboard navigation, both styles (Reef map and Graph), light and dark, and `file://` as well as a web server. The default layout is identical to v2.11.0, bubble for bubble. Still one hand-written page with no library and no network; the map's data is identical to v2.11.0, and the browser keeps only the style, the trail and whether the hint was closed. The claims, the 90 things and their mentions (the precision review of the sample still stands at 0 wrong in 100), the kits, the agent pack apart from its version, and all PDFs (byte-identical to v2.11.0). Two rebuilds give identical files.
- **Checks.** A review drove every control with the mouse, touch and keyboard in Deep Dive and Art Deco, light and dark, desktop and phone, on both copies of the map. It fixed four bugs: Fit did not match the opening view, a tap on a grown bubble could hit its small − and collapse it, a double tap low on a phone screen landed on the preview card, and after growing a bubble on a phone the new bubbles could fall off screen. With Search Console's 283 open nodes, a redraw takes 24 ms, and zooming, panning and dragging stay under 35 ms a frame. `test_reef_map` now covers pan and zoom, the preview, dragging, growing, the menu, the trail, the living layout, touch, and Fit equal to the opening view. There are still 68 tests, and all pass.

## v2.10.0 — 2026-10-04
**Phase 2 of the knowledge graph: the Reef map. The things, topics, kit items, documents and claims can now be explored as a map of links, one neighbourhood at a time: put any thing in the centre, see what surrounds it, click a neighbour to move there, and use the browser's back button to return. It is called the Reef map in the Deep Dive style and the Graph in the Art Deco style. No claim, thing or kit item changed.**

- **What it shows.** One node in the centre and its neighbours around it, never the whole graph at once. The things appear as bubbles coloured by kind, topics as larger bubbles, Google's pages and press reports as documents, and requirements, facts, myths, quotes, story angles and glossary terms as squares. Claims appear as a shoal of small dots under the centre. The layout is fixed, ordered by kind, weight and name, so the same node always draws the same map. Line styles tell the kinds of link apart, and a legend explains them. Co-mention lines are thinner and grow with the number of shared claims. A big node stays readable: each kind shows its first few neighbours, and a "+12 more" bubble adds twelve at a time, up to "Show fewer". For example, Search Console has 303 neighbours and opens in about 33 ms. Two hops draw a second ring.
- **The side panel** shows the centre's card and all its neighbours as a plain list, grouped by kind of link. For each of the 90 things, it lists the same neighbours as the thing's card: the claims, Google's pages, kit items, typed relations, co-mentioned things and topics. The build refuses a mismatch. A note repeats the rule: a link is a map, not a source, so cite the claim IDs.
- **Controls.** The page has the same search as the site, with other names and typo tolerance: "GSC" finds Search Console. Filters cover the kinds of node and link, and the claims' day, label and grade. For example, Googlebot's 68 claims split 40 on Day 1 and 19 on Day 2. Hop depth is 1 or 2, and zoom has in, out and fit.
- **How to open it.**
  - In the web edition: **Reef map** in the menu, **Open in Reef map** on every thing's card and every topic page, or `60-outputs/site/graph.html`. It opens on Googlebot.
  - Next to the mindmap: `50-maps/graph.html`. The web edition build writes this copy, with its data inline, so it also works on its own.
  - The address names the centre, such as `graph.html#ent:googlebot` or `graph.html#topic:robots-txt`, so a view can be bookmarked or shared.
- **Keyboard.** Tab moves through the header, the search box, the controls, the centre and then the ring. The arrow keys step around the ring, Home and End jump to the first and last node, and Enter puts the focused node in the centre. "/" jumps to search, whose list of matches works with the arrow keys and Enter. Every control and node has a name for screen readers, and focus is always visible. On a phone (375 px), the map sits above the panel in one column, and tapping a node centres it.
- **Mindmap and map link both ways.** A topic's panel in the mindmap lists its top things (up to eight) under "Things in this topic", each one opening the Reef map on that thing, plus a link that opens the topic itself in the map. The mindmap's header has a Reef map link. The map's topic nodes link back to the mindmap (`mindmap.html#topic:<id>` now opens on that topic) and to the topic page.
- **Offline, as everything else.** One hand-written page (`_system/templates/graph.template.html`) draws the map as plain SVG, with no library, no modules, no `fetch` and no network. Its data, `assets/graph-data.js` (`window.KB_GRAPH`, about 1.2 MB, loaded only by this page), comes from the agent pack's `graph.json`: 3,343 nodes and 17,167 links, with sessions, days and areas left out. The page opens from `file://` without a console error, in Deep Dive and Art Deco, light and dark, desktop and phone. The data holds public claims only: the build's privacy scan covers the new files, and no private claim ID, excluded photo or speaker field is in them.
- **Unchanged.** The claims, the 90 things and their mentions (the precision review of the sample still stands at 0 wrong in 100), the kits, the agent pack apart from its version, and all PDFs. Two rebuilds give identical files.
- **Checks.** A review checked every acceptance point of the plan on the real build, in all four looks and at phone width. It made five fixes: "+ more" now adds twelve at a time, the mindmap lists each topic's things, the pop-in animation stops after 60 nodes, the `50-maps` README lists the Reef map, and the test covers more. One new regression test (`test_reef_map`) loads the map from `file://`, recentres it by click and by keyboard, goes back, checks the filters, hop depth and search, and compares the panel with the card for every thing. That makes 64 tests.

## v2.8.0 — 2026-10-04
**Phase 0 of the knowledge graph: the links that already existed are now visible and walkable. Every claim shows what rests on it, every source and topic shows what it backs, search covers the kits and the glossary and forgives typos, and AI agents get the same links as one graph file. No claim changed and no new data was written by hand.**

- **What rests on a claim.** Every claim card in the web edition, on the topic and session pages and in `claims.jsonl` now ends with "Used by": the developer requirements, facts, myths, quotes, story angles and glossary terms built on it, each one click away. 1,516 of the 2,228 public claims carry at least one, 2,976 links in all, and every list equals the kits' own claim lists.
- **What a source or a topic backs.** The sources page lists, for each of the 241 cited sources, the claims it backs grouped by grade (confirmed, consistent and so on) and, for 128 of them, the developer requirements that cite it. Every one of the 96 topic pages, in the web edition and in the agent pack, has a new section "Built on these claims": the kit items that rest on the topic's claims.
- **Search that finds more and forgives more.** Search now covers the glossary, the topics, the developer requirements, the facts, myths, story angles and quotes, the sessions and the claims, in that order, so typing "noindex" shows the glossary term and DEV-IDX-01 before the claims. It matches word starts ("canon" finds canonical) and corrects typos: "hreflung" shows "Showing results for hreflang (you typed hreflung)" with a button to search the literal word. 187 aliases come from the glossary's own brackets, as in *Crawl rate limit (hostload)*, plus a short list of common abbreviations (GSC, CWV, LCP, INP, AIO, GMC, JS, SSR, CSR, LLM, EEAT, REP, SD).
- **Speaker filter, no person pages.** As the owner decided, there are no pages about people.
