A

AI Mode

Google Search's AI mode, where users ask longer questions (about three times as long as a traditional search on average, a Google slide said; the opening keynote said two to three times) and follow-up questions. It uses the same crawling and index as Search and adds grounding and query fan-out when it answers.

D1-C038 D1-C017 D1-C019

ThingAI Mode

AI Overviews

AI-generated summaries shown in Google results with links to supporting pages. They are built on Google's core ranking systems and use content indexed by Google Search.

D1-C051 D2-C074

ThingAI Overviews

AI slop

Low-quality, mass-produced AI-generated content. Google counts it as scaled content abuse and said it is going to great lengths to keep it out of its results.

D3-C252 D3-C695 D3-C696

AI-powered configuration

An experimental Search Console chat box that turns a natural-language request into Performance report filters, applied only after the user confirms.

D3-C429 D3-C431 D3-C464

Alternate names

The other URLs of a duplicate cluster that Google keeps tracking, which is why an old domain can still appear after a site move.

D2-C352 D2-C357

ARIA

Accessible Rich Internet Applications: attributes, not a programming language, that add accessibility information to HTML, such as role=button and aria-label on a div used as a button. No ARIA is better than bad ARIA, a community speaker warned.

D1-C279 D1-C280

B

Back button hijacking

Interfering with the browser's back button, for example sending users to an auto-generated page instead of where they came from. An explicit violation of Google's malicious practices spam policy, enforced since 15 June 2026.

D3-C208 D3-C209

BERT, RankBrain and MUM

AI systems in Google Search: BERT understands words in the context of the sentence, RankBrain interprets the intent behind queries, and MUM is used only for specific applications, not general ranking. On Day 3 Google called BERT a predictive language model, technically an LLM.

D1-C042 D1-C043 D1-C130 D3-C684

Bidirectional text

Text that mixes right-to-left scripts, such as Persian or Arabic, with left-to-right text, which can display in a confusing order in titles, product names and URLs.

D2-C963 D2-C964

Boosted consumers

Google's term for consumers who hand AI the mental effort of a purchase decision without surrendering the choice, and feel more confident thanks to ease, assistance and suggestion.

D3-C559 D3-C561 D3-C563 D3-C568

Brand heuristic

A cognitive shortcut in which consumers use a brand's name and reputation as a proxy for product quality, reliability and value, especially in the final reassurance step.

D3-C577 D3-C578

Branded queries filter

A Search Console filter that splits branded from non-branded queries with an internal AI-assisted system, not a regular expression, on top-level properties with enough queries.

D3-C473 D3-C499

C

Canonical

The representative URL of a duplicate cluster, the one Google would ideally show; signals such as links are forwarded to it.

D2-C347 D2-C348

ThingCanonicalization

Canonical chain and canonical loop

A chain is a canonical pointing to a URL that leads on to another URL; a loop leads back to where it started. Google can follow chains but recommends pointing straight at the final URL; a community speaker called loops a structural conflict.

D2-C419 D2-C420 D2-C424

cats.txt

A file an SEO created as a satirical take on llms.txt, showing that crawlers fetch whatever files they find. Google said such files have no importance for Google Search.

D1-C472 D1-C473

ccTLD

A country-code top-level domain such as .de, one of the strongest signals of which country a site targets.

D2-C591 D2-C593

Change of Address tool

A Search Console tool for telling Google that a site moved from one domain or subdomain to another; Google's site move guide pairs it with submitting the new sitemap and updating internal links.

D2-C912 D2-C884

Cloaking and doorways

Two spam types in Google's policies: cloaking shows different content to users and to search engines to manipulate rankings; doorways are pages made to rank for similar queries that lead users to less useful intermediate pages.

D3-C278 D3-C279

Commodity content

Content based on very common knowledge that anyone could have written, with little insight or added value, such as a generic top-10 buying-tips list. Non-commodity content offers a unique, expert or experienced take.

D3-C178 D3-C179 D3-C180

Content quality (effort, originality, talent or skill, accuracy)

The four attributes Google's rater guidelines use for main content: the extent to which a human put effort, originality, talent or skill and accuracy into creating it.

D3-C132 D3-C176

Contractual crawlers (special-case crawlers)

Google crawlers that serve a specific product by agreement with the crawled site, and the only Google crawlers that may ignore robots.txt; AdsBot, for example, ignores the global user agent with the ad publisher's permission.

D1-C330 D1-C343

ThingSpecial-case crawlers

Conversational attributes

Merchant Center feed attributes for AI shopping experiences such as AI Mode: question and answer, documents, related products, item group title, variant option and popularity rank. Retailers worldwide can use them since May 2026.

D3-C365 D3-C362 D3-C367

Core update

A significant, broad change to Google's ranking systems, made several times a year and announced on the Google Search Status Dashboard. Core updates do not target specific sites and are not penalties.

D3-C259 D3-C260 D3-C262 D3-C258

ThingCore updates

Crawl budget

How much Google crawls a site: the combination of crawl rate limit and crawl demand, the finite resources Google allocates to crawling one website. A site with fewer than a few thousand URLs likely has no crawl budget problem, Google said.

D1-C090 D1-C096 D1-C373 D1-C381

ThingCrawl budget

Crawl demand

How much Google wants to crawl a site, driven by its quality, how often its URLs change and how popular they are. Demand from other Google products, such as Ads, counts too, a Google panelist said.

D1-C093 D1-C538

ThingCrawl demand

Crawl rate limit (hostload)

How much crawling a host can take, shared by all Google crawlers. It goes down when connect time, time to first byte, 429 or 5xx responses go up. It applies per host, so a site's CDN, subdomains and main www host may be counted separately. A drop is most of the time an abrupt step down, and Googlebot then opens fewer connections, a Google panelist said.

D1-C091 D1-C092 D1-C132 D1-C376 D1-C493 D1-C494

ThingCrawl capacity limit

Crawl scheduler

The part of Google's crawling that hands the crawler an ordered list of URLs from the crawl queue, putting pages that change often first; Google rarely discusses it in public because the details get taken out of context.

D1-C332 D1-C333 D1-C327

Crawl Stats report

A Search Console report, under Settings, that shows how much Google crawls a site, the responses and errors it received, the file types fetched by its crawlers and the split between discovery of new URLs and refresh of known ones.

D1-C339 D1-C340 D1-C393 D1-C396

ThingCrawl stats

Crawled – currently not indexed

A Page indexing report status: Google crawled the page but did not index it; the page does not need to be resubmitted.

D2-C711 D2-C712

Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)

The Core Web Vitals metric for visual stability. A community speaker said layout shifts, such as a button popping in after the first load, annoy users and may confuse AI agents.

D1-C275

D

Discovered – currently not indexed

A Page indexing report status: Google knows the URL but has not crawled it yet.

D2-C707 D2-C706

Downstream impact

The effect SEO work has on other channels, such as direct visits or paid search, which a community speaker advised measuring when AI answers take clicks.

D3-C515 D3-C517

Duplicate cluster

A group of pages Google considers essentially equivalent: it stores one in the index and keeps track of the other URLs.

D2-C346 D1-C128

ThingDuplicate content

E

E-E-A-T

Experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust, from Google's rater guidelines: first-hand experience, the necessary knowledge or skill, and being a go-to source. Trust comes first: untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T however expert they seem.

D3-C222 D3-C223

ThingE-E-A-T

F

Faceted navigation

Filters on listing pages whose combinations create very many URLs, for example the same facets in a different order; Google's guide prefers keeping filter URLs from being crawled.

D1-C100 D1-C101

ThingFaceted navigation

G

Generative AI performance report

Search Console reports, rolled out worldwide by 31 August 2026, that show impressions, pages, countries, devices (Search only) and dates for generative AI features, with no clicks or queries. Since 24 September 2026 they also include multimodal searches; the regular Performance report still counts AI traffic too.

D1-C124 D1-C062 D3-C438 D3-C465

ThingGenerative AI performance report

GEO and AEO

Labels for generative engine optimisation and answer engine optimisation. Google's answer: don't worry about the name, good SEO is good GEO and AEO.

D1-C050 D1-C052

ThingGEO

Google Discover

Google's Discover surface: indexed content that meets Discover's content policies is eligible automatically, with no special tags or structured data, and Discover uses many of the same signals and systems as Search.

D3-C219 D3-C220

ThingDiscover

Google Lens

Google's visual search, which Google said it built after seeing young users upload pictures and expect answers about them; more than 1.5 billion people use it every month, according to Google's May 2025 announcement.

D1-C188 D1-C191

ThingGoogle Lens

Google-Extended

A robots.txt control token, not a separate crawler, that decides whether crawled content may train future Gemini models and ground Gemini apps and Vertex AI. It does not affect inclusion or ranking in Google Search. A site can disallow it for a directory or for the whole site.

D1-C086 D1-C032 D1-C522

ThingGoogle-Extended

Googlebot-Image and Googlebot-Video

The Google crawlers that fetch images and videos; robots.txt rules for their user agent tokens control how Google indexes a site's images and videos.

D2-C931 D2-C932

Grounding

Giving an AI model content from the Google Search index at prompt time, so that its answer rests on that content.

D2-C121 D1-C038

ThingGrounding

H

Hallucination

An AI model making things up when it lacks information or because of issues in its training. Google said it can happen with any model and, with current training methods, cannot be eliminated; grounding reduces it.

D3-C686 D3-C688 D3-C687

History API

The browser feature (pushState) that lets a single-page app use real, clean URLs instead of # fragments, so Google can follow its links.

D2-C285 D2-C288

Honest Results Policy

Google's policy that no website gets an advantage in Search because it is a Google client or partner or has a personal relationship with Google; the Search team gives no site special treatment, and Google does not accept payment to rank websites in organic search.

D1-C178 D1-C179 D1-C143

hreflang

Annotations that tell Google which pages are language or regional versions of each other, so it can show the right one. They do not set a page's language.

D2-C033 D2-C564 D2-C584

Thinghreflang

hreflang return link

The rule that if page X names page Y as a language version, page Y must name page X back; Google ignores annotations that point only one way.

D2-C565 D2-C570

HTTP status code classes

1xx informational, 2xx success, 3xx redirection, 4xx client error and 5xx server error. Each class affects crawling differently, and 429 is the 4xx code that tells Google to slow down instead of saying there is nothing at the URL.

D1-C345 D1-C353

Things4xx 5xx

Hub pages

Pages such as the homepage or category pages that link out to new or updated pages. Google may visit them more often, and recommended them over HTML sitemaps.

D1-C202 D2-C838

I

Index selection

The last step before Google's index: it calculates thresholds and decides which documents are kept, using the signals calculated earlier, such as quality, language, country and spam.

D2-C682 D2-C688 D2-C687

ThingIndex selection

J

JSON-LD

The structured data syntax Google recommends: one contiguous block that is easier to write correctly. Microdata and RDFa are interpreted the same way.

D2-C495 D2-C494

ThingJSON-LD

L

Lazy loading

Loading content only when it is needed. Triggered by an Intersection Observer it can work for Google; triggered by scroll events or clicks it does not.

D2-C198 D2-C197 D2-C268

ThingLazy loading

llms.txt

A text file some sites add for AI systems. Google says it isn't necessary: Google Search ignores such AI text files, so they neither help nor harm. On Day 1 Google said llms.txt does not matter for Google Search right now and that it does not expect it to.

D1-C054 D1-C061 D1-C454

Thingllms.txt

Lost in the middle

A pattern research has found in large language models, which a community speaker linked to the serial-position effect in human memory: information in the middle of a text is used less than at the start or end. Google has not said its systems behave this way.

D1-C414 D1-C417

M

Main content (centerpiece)

The part of a page that serves its purpose, as opposed to header, navigation and footer: any part that directly helps the page achieve its purpose, including images, video, tools and user-generated content. Google identifies it and treats it as the most important part of the page.

D2-C310 D2-C028 D2-C862 D2-C870

ThingMain content

max-image-preview

A robots rule for the largest image preview Google may show: none, standard or large, for example in Discover.

D2-C087

Thingmax-image-preview

max-snippet

A robots rule that caps a snippet at a number of characters: 0 means no snippet and -1 means no limit. It also limits how much of the page AI features may use.

D2-C082 D2-C728

Thingmax-snippet

Merchant Center

Google's tool through which merchants send product data feeds for Shopping ads, Search ads and organic results. Products need it to appear in the Shopping tab; product rich results do not.

D3-C352 D3-C346 D3-C343

ThingMerchant Center

Messy middle

Google's model, used since 2019, of what happens between the trigger of a purchase journey and the purchase: people loop between exploring and evaluating until they feel confident enough to buy.

D3-C546 D3-C547 D3-C549

Multimodal search type

A Search Console search type, since September 2026, for searches made with images: Google Lens, Circle to Search, images uploaded to Google Search and Chrome's image search.

D3-C458 D3-C459 D3-C465

N

nofollow

A hint not to follow links or pass signals through them; as a page-level robots rule it covers every link on the page. It does not block crawling.

D1-C127 D2-C063

Thingnofollow

noimageindex

A robots meta rule that tells Google not to index the images on a page. Google said it also affects the page's videos, because a video's thumbnail is an image.

D2-C934 D2-C935

noindex

A robots rule that tells Google not to show a page in search results. It works only if Google can crawl the page and see the rule.

D2-C061 D2-C022

Thingnoindex

nosnippet and data-nosnippet

nosnippet stops text snippets and video previews for a page and keeps its content out of AI Overviews and AI Mode as a direct input; the data-nosnippet attribute hides only a marked part of the page.

D2-C070 D2-C072 D2-C076

Thingsdata-nosnippet nosnippet

P

PageRank and BackRub

PageRank is the link-analysis algorithm Google's founders invented; BackRub was their 1995 Stanford search engine, soon renamed Google. Google's ranking systems guide says PageRank has evolved and is still part of its core ranking systems.

D3-C231 D3-C233 D3-C138

ThingPageRank

Platform properties

Search Console properties for Instagram, TikTok, X or YouTube accounts, verified through a pop-up from the platform, that show how the account's content performs on Google Search, Discover and Google News.

D3-C443 D3-C446 D3-C467

ThingPlatform properties

Posting list

A classic retrieval structure: for a token, the list of URLs that contain it. Google intersects the lists of a query's important words to find candidate pages.

D2-C732 D2-C733 D2-C736

Product highlights and product details

Two existing Merchant Center attributes Google now emphasises for AI: product_highlight, short bullets about the product itself, and product_detail, specifications as section, name and value.

D3-C392 D3-C393 D3-C395

Prompt journalism

A community speaker's name for journalistic and content work, such as interviews and podcasts, done with a prompt-assisted workflow that optimises material already recorded rather than creating more content.

D2-C953

Q

Query fan-out

Running several related searches at once to gather more results for an AI answer; a question about lawn weeds may also search herbicides and weed prevention. In Google's AI features an LLM generates the fan-out queries, and each goes through the same query understanding as a typed query; Google said they are not added to Search Console.

D1-C053 D2-C729 D3-C058 D3-C062 D3-C063

ThingQuery fan-out

Query groups

Search Console groups of queries with very similar intent written differently, computed with AI and titled with the most prominent query. Shown in Search Console Insights for properties with many queries; they do not affect ranking.

D3-C413 D3-C422 D3-C463

ThingQuery groups

Query understanding

The first serving step: Google detects a query's language, separates its words, drops stop words where they matter little, recognises entities and expands the query with synonyms before looking it up. Google said it rewrites pretty much every query.

D3-C006 D3-C012 D3-C016 D3-C028

ThingQuery understanding

R

Raw HTML and rendered HTML

The raw HTML is what the server sends; the rendered HTML is the page after its JavaScript has run. Google's testing tools show the rendered HTML, and differences between the two point to rendering problems.

D2-C176 D2-C215

Redirect chain

A series of redirects from one URL to the next. Google's crawlers follow up to 10 hops by default; robots.txt is checked for every URL in the chain, and Search Console reports a block on the first URL, a community speaker said.

D1-C370 D1-C534 D1-C535

Redirect map

The migration document that gives every old URL an approved destination; a community speaker also recorded, per URL, what must survive, who approves and the test that proves it works.

D2-C895 D2-C899

rel=canonical

A link element in the head of a page that names the site owner's preferred URL. Google treats it as a strong signal, not an order.

D2-C032 D2-C391 D2-C380

Thingrel=canonical

Render queue

Where pages wait to be rendered after crawling: a few seconds, but it can take longer, and Google documents no upper limit. On Day 3 Google estimated hours in the queue and said everything in it gets rendered within weeks.

D2-C170 D2-C172 D3-C625 D3-C629

Rendering

Running a page's JavaScript in a browser engine to see what users see. Google renders with Chromium and indexes the rendered HTML.

D2-C112 D2-C126 D2-C131

ThingRendering

Retrieval

The serving step that looks up the query's words and their expansions in the index's posting lists and orders the candidates, by language, country and quality among other signals, before ranking starts.

D3-C073 D3-C075 D3-C079 D3-C083

Review snippet

A rich result with an average star rating and often the number of reviews, from Review structured data. Self-serving reviews of a site's own business are not eligible.

D3-C337 D3-C340

RFC 9309 (Robots Exclusion Protocol)

The IETF standard for robots.txt, officially the Robots Exclusion Protocol (REP), supported by basically all major crawlers. It controls which automated clients may access which URLs, not how the content is used, and parsers must skip lines they cannot parse.

D1-C512 D1-C513 D1-C518

Rich results

Search results with extra visual features, such as review stars or recipe filters, usually powered by structured data. AI Overviews and AI Mode are not rich results.

D2-C454 D2-C521 D3-C324 D3-C325

ThingRich results

Rich Results Test

Google's tool to test and preview structured data: it shows the rich result features it detected and whether the markup is valid. It tests either a URL or a pasted code snippet.

D2-C498 D1-C506

ThingRich Results Test

Robots meta tag

An HTML tag in the head of a page that gives search engines page-specific rules, such as noindex or nosnippet, for indexing and display in results.

D2-C054 D2-C056 D2-C057

ThingRobots meta tag

robots.txt

A file in the root of each host (example.com/robots.txt) that tells crawlers which URLs they may fetch. It controls crawling, not indexing, applies only to its own host and is not a security measure: anyone can read it.

D1-C084 D2-C058 D2-C209 D1-C515 D1-C514

Thingrobots.txt

robots.txt report

A Search Console report that shows the robots.txt file as Google last fetched it, with a version history and the errors and successes of each fetch. Google said it uses Google's open-source robots.txt parser.

D1-C528 D1-C530

Thingrobots.txt report

S

Scaled content abuse

A Google spam policy against generating many pages mainly to manipulate rankings with little value to users, however they are made, including automated translation of scraped content.

D2-C611 D1-C129

ThingScaled content abuse

Schema.org

The shared markup vocabulary that major search engines announced together in June 2011; Google uses only the subsets its documentation declares.

D2-C484 D2-C487

ThingSchema.org

Search agents (information agents)

AI Mode agents set up by typing a natural-language request, which AI Mode interprets to create an alert; Google said they launched globally a day or two before 30 September 2026.

D1-C194 D1-C192

Search Console bulk data export

The export of Search Console performance data to BigQuery, the most complete query dataset available, with anonymized queries excluded, a community speaker's slide said.

D3-C475 D3-C471

Search features

Elements of the results page beyond plain text results, built as the last serving step after ranking, mostly from what Google extracted at indexing. Rich results are the features that usually need structured data; AI Overviews and AI Mode are not rich results.

D3-C322 D3-C323 D3-C324 D3-C325

Search generative AI control

A Search Console property setting (inherit, include or exclude) that controls whether a site appears in AI Overviews, AI Mode and generative AI features in Discover, without affecting web rankings.

D1-C029 D1-C030 D1-C031

ThingSearch generative AI control

Search interest

Google Trends' measure: the share of all searches for a term, on a 0 to 100 scale. It is not search volume.

D2-C784

ThingSearch interest

Search profile

A profile publishers and creators can claim in Google Search, with name, photo, bio, links and pinned posts, which users can follow. In the US, 10,000 followers across YouTube, Instagram, X or TikTok qualify since 16 September 2026. Its verified accounts are added automatically as Search Console platform properties.

D1-C025 D1-C125 D3-C468

ThingSearch profiles

Search quality raters

External reviewers who rate results for changes Google is testing, following the Search Quality Rater Guidelines. They cannot affect individual sites' rankings, and nothing in their guidelines is itself a ranking factor.

D3-C159 D3-C160 D3-C163 D3-C168

ThingSearch quality raters

SHACL

A rule language for validating structured data. Google announced downloadable SHACL rules for each feature as coming soon.

D2-C514 D2-C516

Shadow DOM

A browser feature that isolates a component's styles. Google flattens shadow DOM content when it renders a page, so the content can be indexed if it appears in the rendered HTML.

D2-C228 D2-C235

Side-by-side and live traffic experiments

Google's ways of testing a Search change: raters compare results with and without the change side by side, and a change that does well then runs on a small slice of live traffic, usually starting at 0.1% of people.

D3-C151 D3-C156 D3-C155

Similar URLs

Technically different URLs that differ in only a few characters, such as protocol or host, capitalisation, delimiters, the encoding of a space or the order of parameters; a community speaker treated them as a sign of duplicate content and other technical issues.

D1-C398 D1-C399 D1-C397

Single-page app (SPA)

A JavaScript application that changes views without full page reloads. Typical search risks: links without real URLs, # routes and error views that return 200.

D2-C183 D2-C186 D2-C190 D2-C288

ThingSingle-page app

Site name

The name Google shows for a site in results, an attribution feature rather than a rich result. Google generates it automatically, and a site can indicate its preferred name with WebSite structured data on its home page.

D3-C335 D3-C336

Soft 404

A page that returns a success code (200) while its content looks like an error or an empty page. Google keeps it out of the index but may keep crawling it. A CDN captcha page served with 200 is a common cause.

D1-C073 D2-C334 D1-C367

ThingSoft 404

Spam update

A notable improvement to Google's automated spam detection systems, such as SpamBrain, listed with its ranking updates. Spam updates target spam pages; to recover, a site removes the spam Google described.

D3-C287 D3-C203 D3-C289

ThingSpam updates

SpamBrain

Google's AI-based spam prevention system, launched in 2018 and continuously improved since; Google said it is now built on Gemini.

D2-C671 D2-C669

ThingSpamBrain

Stop words

Words such as 'a' and 'of' that Google removes from a query when it thinks they matter little; where they matter, Google tries to recognise the whole phrase, stop words included, as an entity.

D3-C012 D3-C014

Storebot-Google

Google Shopping's own crawler. It crawls more often than Googlebot, goes through product, cart and checkout pages to verify merchant data, and robots.txt rules for it affect all Google Shopping surfaces.

D3-C385 D3-C388 D3-C389

ThingStorebot-Google

Structured data

Machine-readable markup that describes a page's content, so Google understands it more accurately and the page can become eligible for rich results.

D2-C455 D2-C521

ThingStructured data

Synonyms and siblings

Synonyms are words people use interchangeably, which Google adds to a query automatically even when they are not synonyms linguistically; siblings are related words in the same category that are not interchangeable, such as Canon and Nikon, so a search for one should not show the other.

D3-C025 D3-C036 D3-C038 D3-C039

T

Tokenization

Splitting text into small units. Google Search stores words (tokens) with metadata, while AI models split text into sub-word pieces mapped to numeric IDs.

D2-C318 D2-C326 D2-C327

ThingTokenization

Transformer

The neural network architecture of the paper 'Attention Is All You Need', the T in ChatGPT, which Google said it created and published to move the industry forward, competitors included.

D1-C161

Transliterated queries

Searches typed in Latin letters for a language written in another script, such as Persian or Hindi, with the same intent as a search in the native script; Google says a Hindi searcher typing in Latin letters may want and get Hindi results written either way.

D2-C965 D2-C981

Trending now

The Google Trends tab that shows what people are searching for on Google right now, with approximate, bucketed search volumes.

D2-C765 D2-C768

ThingTrending now

U

unavailable_after

A robots rule that lets a page drop out of search results after a set date and time.

D2-C100 D2-C698

Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP)

An open standard for agentic commerce, covering discovery, buying and post-purchase support, launched by Google on 11 January 2026 with retailers and platforms.

D3-C398 D3-C397

Universal search

Google's 2007 launch that blended results from Images, Maps, Books, Video and News into web results, so the results page mixes result types.

D3-C307 D3-C306

URL Inspection tool

Search Console's tool that shows how Google sees a URL, including the rendered HTML; its live test can differ from the indexed version.

D2-C176 D2-C178

ThingURL Inspection

User-agent group

In robots.txt, one or more user-agent lines followed by their allow and disallow rules. A crawler follows only the most specific group that names it, so groups do not add up; Google combines all groups that name the same user agent.

D1-C519 D1-C080 D1-C533 D1-C527

User-triggered fetchers

Fetchers that request a page because a user asked for it, such as a translation service. Google's documentation says they generally ignore robots.txt, and Google said AI agents acting for a user fall into the same category.

D1-C273 D1-C434 D1-C435

ThingUser-triggered fetchers

V

Vector embeddings

Numeric representations of parts of documents and of queries that form a vector space. Besides posting lists, Google can retrieve documents by how close their embeddings are to the query's embedding.

D2-C740 D2-C741

Video sitemap

A sitemap or feed that tells Google which URLs carry videos, so it can visit them without checking every page; Google said it ingests video sitemaps much more often than it can process HTML pages.

D2-C925 D2-C926 D2-C556

Video watch page

A page dedicated to one video. Google said a video without one is not going to be indexed, and its Video indexing report help says only videos on a watch page are eligible for indexing.

D2-C916 D2-C942

Viewport expansion

How Google sees content below the first screen without scrolling: it renders the page like a very long phone (around 10,000 pixels high, Google said at the event).

D2-C273 D2-C272

W

Web Guide

A Google Search experiment, introduced in Search Labs in July 2025, that uses a custom version of Gemini and query fan-out to group web results into categories with a short summary for each.

D3-C707 D3-C712

WebMCP

Web Model Context Protocol: a proposed web standard that lets a site declare the actions it offers AI agents, through declarative HTML annotations or imperative tools. Chrome offers it for testing through an origin trial or a flag.

D1-C281 D1-C283 D1-C316

X

X-Robots-Tag

An HTTP header that carries the same rules as the robots meta tag, also for files that are not HTML. Like the tag, Google only sees it on URLs it is allowed to crawl.
