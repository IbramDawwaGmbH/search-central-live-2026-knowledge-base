Topic · Search trends and Google Trends

Using Google Trends for SEO and content

Google presented three uses of Google Trends for content, marketing and SEO: keyword selection, scheduling and ideation. For keyword selection, compare candidate terms on the Explore page for the right country, region and time frame, use the higher-interest wording in titles and campaigns, and check both top and rising commonly searched queries. For scheduling, check whether your topics are seasonal in your own market rather than assuming (a five-year US chart showed umbrella interest peaking in June with sunscreen, not in winter), because a seasonal pattern predicts the next wave of interest and the time to have content ready. For ideation, Trending now surfaces events within about ten minutes, and Google said journalists use it every morning to find content gaps (not in Google's docs); Google's own Trends guide adds to follow trends only where the topic fits the business and its expertise. A Day 2 community talk made the same point for international sites: regional demand changes over time, as a chart of northern versus southern European interest in ceiling fans showed. On Day 3 a community speaker described using Trends to choose what to cover in depth when entering a new market, and Google's marketing research showed Trends charts of trust-checking searches such as 'is [brand] legit' rising since about 2020 in many markets and languages (said at the event).

What to do

  • Compare candidate keywords in Trends for the exact country or region where the page should rank, and reuse the higher-interest wording in titles and headings.
  • Map seasonality per target market and publish or refresh seasonal content a few weeks before the rise.
  • Use rising queries to find emerging sub-topics worth a new page or section.
  • Check Trending now for content gaps, but cover only trends that fit your business and expertise.
  • Re-check regional demand for core product categories at least once a year.
  • After a launch or campaign, compare baseline interest before and after the spike.
  • Track trust queries such as '[brand] legit' or '[brand] seriös' per market in Trends and Search Console, and answer them on the site.
