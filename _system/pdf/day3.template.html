Summary

Key takeaways

Day 3 followed a query from the search box to the results page, then to what site owners can measure. Quality came up at nearly every step: at retrieval, in ranking and in core updates.
  1. Google rewrites pretty much every query.Synonym expansion is traditional Search, not AI. Google knows the synonyms: don't list them on pages.
  2. Fan-out queries are ordinary queries.Google treats them like typed ones. They are not in Search Console; don't chase individual ones.
  3. Quality decides retrieval, not just ranking.The most important signal for ordering candidates, used again in ranking. Weak pages may never compete.
  4. Quality means human effort.Effort, originality, talent or skill, and accuracy. Quality issues are quality issues, not AI versus human.
  5. Core updates are not penalties.No recovery in the penalty sense: improve the site as a whole, and allow several months.
  6. AI Overviews and AI Mode are not rich results.No special markup: a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown with a snippet.
  7. Product data feeds AI shopping.For deeper AI Mode questions: six new Merchant Center attributes. Rich results need only markup; the Shopping tab needs Merchant Center.
  8. The generative AI report counts impressions only.No clicks, no queries; the Performance report still includes AI traffic. Measure visits yourself.
  9. Clicks no longer tell the whole story.Google advises judging the value of visits, such as sales and sign-ups. Report conversions too.
  10. AI "boosts" shoppers; it doesn't choose for them.People hand AI the effort, then double-check on Search and brand sites; familiar brands reassure.
  11. Discovery is slow, indexing is fast.Google's averages: about 20 hours to discover a URL, 30 days to recrawl it, 1.5 hours to index it.
  12. AI SEO is SEO.AI features use exactly the same processes as traditional results, with very few of their own.

Section 02 · Making sense of users' queries · John Mueller

Query understanding

Google rewrites pretty much every query before it looks anything up: it detects the language, drops stop words, recognises entities and adds synonyms. Fan-out queries from the AI features go through the same steps.

Language, words and entities

Stage For almost any query, Google first detects the language, which tells it roughly what content the user wants. A brand name alone doesn't reveal it, so Google falls back on location and browser settings and sometimes shows the wrong language version. Docs Search Help adds the Google language setting and the device's languages.

Analysis Don't leave brand searches to chance: connect language versions with hreflang and give every page a visible language switcher.

Stage Next, Google separates the words, harder in languages such as Thai that put no spaces between them, and drops stop words such as "a" and "of", mirroring indexing. Where small words matter, the whole phrase can be recognised as an entity, and a query naming an entity is read as a request for that thing. Ranking matches words or entities and "probably" mixes both. (not in docs)

Synonyms: pretty much every query is rewritten

Stage Google's ranking teams "say that the synonym system is one of the most important parts", because users phrase things differently from the way content is indexed. In Mueller's words, "pretty much all queries are rewritten", in traditional Search, not only in the AI features. Docs How Search Works: the synonym system matches "change laptop brightness" with "adjust laptop brightness".

Stage Synonyms are learned automatically from how people search, not catalogued by linguists: "we don't need to be technically accurate". Words people use interchangeably count as synonyms, which is why a highlighted word in the results can look wrong (not in docs).

Don't write for the synonym system

Stage Google adds synonyms itself, so "you don't have to do that SEO meme of, like, list all the synonyms on your page". It works much as if the user had joined them with OR, so an OR search of a term's synonyms probably returns much the same results (not in docs). "I wouldn't artificially just stuff those variations in there."

Docs The SEO Starter Guide: anticipate readers' words ("charcuterie" or "cheese board") without worrying about every variation. Analysis Drop synonym blocks; lists of variants read as keyword stuffing.

Stage Google does not prefer English, and a page needs no English term where Google knows its local equivalent. For diacritics, foreign words and spellings, focus on what users search: Hindi users search in Hindi script and in Latin letters (not in docs).

Analysis · Test before you write

Search each variant of a key term. Same entity or near-identical results: use your audience's term. Unrelated results: write that variant on the page. Where users type two scripts or spellings, use the forms Search Console's queries show.

Fan-out queries are queries too

Slide "How LLM features with grounding generally work": results go to an LLM, which generates fan-out queries back to the search engine and answers with links. Stage Fan-out queries are treated like typed ones, synonyms included, and kept distinct from each other for coverage.

Stage "Fan-out queries are not added in Search Console, because they're basically a part of our infrastructure." (not in docs) They show up in Gemini and some search APIs. Docs Gemini's grounding responses list the queries run. Analysis Search Console counts AI features under the user's own query.

Stage "I would not overfocus on which individual fan-out queries happen and how can I rank for those". Analysis Cover a topic's real subquestions on solid pages instead.

Section 03 · Making sense of users' queries, part 2 · Gary Illyes

Retrieval: from query to candidates

Retrieval looks the expanded query up in posting lists and already orders the candidates, by language, country and quality, before ranking starts. Google called quality the most important of the three.

Stage The expanded query goes to the index, which keeps posting lists: for each word, the URLs associated with it (see Day 2). Retrieval matches the query's words and their expansions against those lists (not in docs), using signals attached to each document. The first condition: the query's words, or its concepts in the case of embeddings, are in the document or related to it.

Stage A query like [best fried chicken ever] matches millions of pages, so Google already orders the candidates during retrieval, before ranking starts (not in docs).

SignalWhat Google said Stage
1 · LanguageIndexing signals order the candidates, language and country first. A Spanish searcher doesn't necessarily want Italian results; no results in a language is an opportunity.
2 · CountryA searcher in Switzerland wants Swiss cheese, not German. Docs Geotargeting one country can lift rankings there at the expense of other locales.
3 · QualityThe most important: "if the quality of a URL is high, then it's more likely to be retrieved from the index for specific queries." (not in docs) The same signal is used again in ranking.
Analysis · Quality acts twice

A low-quality page is less likely to be retrieved at all, so it may never reach ranking: quality is a precondition, not a final tweak. The docs list quality among ranking signals but never say it decides retrieval. Language and country act just as early, so make both unambiguous: one main language per page, hreflang between versions, local prices, addresses and shipping.

Section 04 · How Google thinks about Quality · Lightning session K · Discover

What quality means to Google

Google defined quality as the effort, originality, talent or skill and accuracy a person put into content, and called it one of the most important of hundreds of ranking signals. A community talk argued that SEO is for humans, and the Discover talk put trust first.

Not one system, but hundreds of signals

Stage Quality sits in the core ranking systems as a signal of its own, a number calculated from many parts (not in docs): one of the most important, but one among hundreds ("quality" is a repaired speech-to-text reading). "Big takeaway: always remember, there's hundreds of ranking signals." For news, freshness, diversity and originality probably weigh more. Analysis Improve content, links, page experience and freshness together, not one metric.

Stage PageRank, the speaker's favourite, "even though we don't really use them so much anymore" (not in docs). Docs It has evolved and is still a core system. Analysis It weighs less, but links still matter. And per the guide MUM is not used for general ranking: the slide lists systems Google runs.

Quality, defined: four attributes

"Quality is basically the extent to which a human being put effort, originality, talent, skill, and accuracy into creating content."Google's quality talk, citing page 21 of the rater guidelines
Effort
Stage How much a human actively worked to create satisfying content.
Originality
Stage Found nowhere else or, if similar content exists, adding more value.
Talent or skill
Stage Enough to give users a satisfying experience.
Accuracy
Stage Above all on YMYL and health topics.

Docs Google's helpful-content guidance lists the same four attributes, and says generative AI used to mass-produce text without oversight represents little to no effort. Analysis Google updated that page on 1 October 2026, during the event, to match the talk: cite it, not the talk. The rater guidelines ask whether a page is the original source: aim for that, and add substantial value where you build on others.

Lightning session K: SEO is for humans

Stage A community speaker: "SEO is for humans". Around 2009 writers bent titles to hit their CMS's keyword-density score, and it worked at the time (not in docs). Docs Today that is keyword stuffing, and Google has no preferred word count. Analysis Plug-in scores are not editorial targets.

Stage Entering Poland late with a small team, a large Spanish software-download site went deep instead of broad, and its Polish edition reached the top three Polish technology sites and double the organic traffic of its English edition (period not stated).

A community case study: niche beats broad

Stage A complete, well-written article got 1,600,000 impressions; one for a very specific audience (friends looking for an alternative to a failing game server) got 46,000 in the same period, yet 28 times more clicks, and its visitors converted 10 times more. Analysis No source or absolute numbers were given, so treat it as an illustration, not a benchmark.

Stage "walk out of your shop. Stand on the other side of the street, look at the shop the way your users are looking at it". If you can't say what problems your pages solve, you are optimising for the search engine.

Discover: trust first

Docs Discover uses many of the same signals and systems as Search. Slide E-E-A-T: first-hand experience, the knowledge or skill, and being a go-to source; and "untrustworthy pages will have low E-E-A-T no matter how Experienced, Expert or Authoritative they may seem".

Slide Images at least 1,200 px wide, over 300,000 pixels, 16x9, enabled by max-image-preview:large; no site logos, text-heavy or misleading images. Docs Name the image in schema.org markup or og:image; news sources need clear dates, bylines, author and publisher details and contact information.

Analysis On article templates: max-image-preview:large, a relevant 1,200 px image in og:image, bylines and dates.

Section 05 · What are quality updates

Core updates and spam

Google changes Search thousands of times a year. Core updates reassess quality across the web and punish no one; spam updates target spam only, and only they come with a recovery in the usual sense.

Why Search changes

Slide Three reasons: new content formats, which may get Search features once enough people look for them (not in docs); a broader web, where the best content is harder to find (core updates); and spammers finding loopholes (targeted algorithms).

Stage The speaker's own label for feature launches is "feature updates": "It's not an official name." Every update has one goal: better results. Docs Google's first index held 26 million pages in 1998. Analysis Quote that, not the rounded 25 million said on stage. Stage For [durian], each result of 2000 faces about 20,000 in 2025 (not in docs).

Section 06 · How Search results are born · Gary Illyes

Search features and rich results

Search features are built as the last serving step, after ranking, and most need nothing from the site owner. Rich results are the exception, usually built from structured data; AI Overviews and AI Mode are not rich results.

Stage Gary Illyes called the results page an auction where result types bid for space (a metaphor, not ads). Text results are the most common type (not in docs).

Query understanding picks the result types

Stage Query understanding predicts intent, and with it the result types. [orange pictures] names the type; for an ambiguous [orange], Google looks at how users reacted to result types for similar queries. Docs Aggregated, anonymized interaction data becomes signals for its machine-learned systems.

Stage From 2005–2006 people added "pictures" to queries, which led to universal search (not in docs). Docs Launched 16 May 2007. Analysis The launch post cites other reasons. Stage An image query is broken into embeddings searched for in the index, and its intent can still be detected (not in docs).

Anatomy of a text result

Stage Title links and snippets come from Google's understanding of the page, even when the owner adds nothing. Image results come from the image index (not in docs); video thumbnails and attribution from the page or its markup.

Search features come last; rich results need data

Slide Search Features is the last step on Google's serving diagram. Stage Results come back from the index and are ranked; then the features are built, mostly from what Google extracted at indexing, with nothing needed from the owner. Rich results are the exception: "The magic behind rich results is usually structured data. Emphasis on 'usually'." Usually JSON-LD.

AI features are not rich results

Stage "AI Mode and AI Overviews are not rich results." They work from the normal text results; only future parts such as images or recipes would need structured data. Docs No special schema.org markup: a page must be indexed and eligible for a snippet.

Analysis Day 2 said processed markup also feeds these features: markup is no condition for appearing, but its data can still reach them.

Articles, site names and reviews

Slide Article markup: "Not required for ‘Top Stories,’ but highly recommended for all articles." It can also flag paywalled content. The slide showed the NewsArticle example from Google's docs (redrawn here). Docs It tells Google explicitly that the page is a news article, its author and its title.

Stage The site name is an attribution feature, not a rich result. Docs Google generates it; state a preferred name with WebSite markup on the home page.

Section 07 · Shopping on Search · Alex Jansen, Google Shopping

Product data for AI shopping

AI Mode answers deeper product questions than classic shopping results, so Google added a few flexible Merchant Center attributes rather than hundreds of narrow ones. Product data quality matters more than ever, for AI and for agents that buy on a user's behalf.

Stage Google Shopping gets billions of product offers from Merchant Center feeds and from schema.org product markup on websites (that part not in docs); both power product snippets in Search. Classic results have little room, so a few attributes were enough: title, image, price, perhaps ratings, shipping and returns. Behind them, brand and barcode (UPC) drive query understanding, retrieval and ranking, and still matter as much.

Stage AI Mode must return far more detail, as shoppers keep asking deeper follow-up questions. Data quality matters most for agents that act for a consumer: "If the agent has bad data, it cannot do the right things." It might buy the wrong product (the agent point not in docs).

Six new attributes, not hundreds

Stage After merchants asked whether AI experiences represented them correctly, Google added feed attributes in 2026, announced at Google Marketing Live. It rejected hundreds of thousands of narrow ones, such as heel height or lens details: hard work for merchants and no longer needed for LLMs (not in docs). Docs Google announced "dozens of new data attributes" on 11 January 2026 for a small group of retailers, and opened conversational attributes to retailers globally on 20 May 2026.

Stage "rich, lightly structured information is critical to AI systems": some structure, no deep nesting, and rich data no standard prescribes, because the merchant knows best (not in docs).

Feeds first; schema.org follows

Stage Most new attributes were said to exist in schema.org already, so Google can read them from markup too, and a recent schema.org release added specification and valueGroup for product details (not in docs). Google works to make what feeds and the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) express expressible in schema.org. Docs UCP, launched on 11 January 2026, is an open standard for agentic commerce, compatible with A2A, AP2 and MCP.

The feed is the documented route

Analysis On 3 October 2026 neither the Merchant Center help nor Search Central documented schema.org markup for the other new attributes, and schema.org's release 30.1 (16 September 2026) is not Google documentation. Send them in the feed.

Analysis Developers: keep price, availability, brand and GTIN accurate first; then add Q&As, highlights, key-value product details and related products in the feed, and mirror them in markup only as Google documents support. Stage Google called this early days.

Analysis Content teams: fill the Q&A attribute from real customer questions (support tickets, reviews, sales chats), answered factually per product.

1 · Query groups

Stage Query groups put together queries with the same intent in different words: more or fewer words, or spelling errors. Slide "How to spell Britney Spears?" Stage Google's logs show hundreds of ways to write it. Grouping goes beyond matching text; granularity is the hard part, and some queries aren't understood or land in illogical groups (that part not in docs).

Stage Groups follow meaning: "Gemini" meant the star sign in 2023 and may be grouped differently now. A click on a group opens the Performance report with a regex filter of all its queries. Analysis The archive page on the slide lists 593 spellings (about 2011). The launch was placed in December on stage; Google's post is dated 27 October 2025. Read groups as intent themes: write for the intent, not each spelling.

How AI changes purchase decisions

Google's marketing research found that shoppers who use AI Mode or AI Overviews feel more confident: they hand AI the effort of a decision, not the choice, and still come back to Search and brand websites to double-check.

Stage Google has used the "messy middle" model since 2019 for the stretch between a purchase's trigger and the purchase. People loop between exploring the category, its prices, brands and options, and evaluating them until confident enough to buy, torn between gathering more information and the effort of processing it. Section 11 · How long does it take to..? · Gary Illyes

How long Google's processes take

Google shared estimates from its internal logs of how long its processes take, as information rather than targets. Finding and recrawling pages takes far longer than indexing them, and every step waits for the one before.

Stage An experiment: estimates from internal logs, informative, nothing to obsess about. Slide Each chart showed a minimum, a typical value, an end point and dotted corner cases, with the footnote "Times expressed on the diagram are estimations based on internal analysis."

Crawling: finding and refreshing URLs

Stage Google knows hundreds of trillions of URLs (October 2026) (not in docs) and crawls far fewer: a smaller crawl space is enough. URLs left unrefreshed for a very long time are dropped; news sites can be refreshed in seconds (not in docs). Analysis Against the "hundreds of billions" of indexed pages in Google's ranking systems guide, about one known URL in a thousand is indexed, taken at face value.

Stage On one audience member's very large site, the homepage was crawled about five times a day and soft-404 clusters every 160 to 190 days. Analysis Link deep pages from strong hubs; check recrawl by depth in logs.

Slide A useful sitemap of a high-quality site is refetched within 14 days at most (not in docs); a lower-quality site's maybe never. Stage robots.txt: a 24-hour objective, per RFC 9309; submitting it in Search Console forces a refresh. Docs The same.

Stage On 500 errors Google cuts crawl capacity within about four hours; increases take one to three weeks, and every site's capacity is recalculated within a month (that part not in docs). Buzz about a few URLs raises crawl demand for the whole site. Docs Many 500, 503 or 429 replies lower the crawl rate until they stop. Analysis A 5xx burst costs hours to cause and weeks to repair.

Rendering and indexing

Stage Rendering happens right after crawling or later in a queue; JavaScript content reaches indexing in hours, at worst weeks; the queue clears within weeks (not in docs). Gary Illyes: "we keep saying that we render every single URL on the internet. I would say that that's not true" (not in docs).

Analysis Google's JavaScript guide says every 200 page is queued for rendering; queued is not rendered. Put critical content, links and meta tags in the server HTML.

Stage Meta annotations such as robots meta tags are a critical step: indexing waits for them (not in docs). Slide "Indexing end to end" ends when a document reaches the serving index, tokenized (not in docs). Analysis Being found takes far longer than being indexed: for speed, link new pages from often-crawled ones and keep sitemaps accurate.

Section 12 · Wrapping all up · Gary Illyes

AI in Search, used responsibly

The closing talk put AI back into proportion: machine learning has run in Search for decades, hallucinations can't be removed, and AI features use the same processes as classic results, so AI SEO is just SEO.

Stage The closing message: use AI, but understand it well enough to use it responsibly. AI is an umbrella of technologies, one of which is machine learning, systems that learn from large amounts of data (not in docs). Machine learning is about 50 years old; Google has used it for "probably 30 years", since the statistical models behind "Did you mean" (not in docs). Some ranking features are plain machine learning models, which beat LLMs at some tasks.

Stage LLMs are deep learning on internet-scale data, mapping concepts by context in a vector space; predictive models guess the next word, generative ones produce output from a prompt (not in docs). BERT, long used in Search, is technically an LLM.

Hallucinations and deception

Stage Any model can hallucinate, and current training can't stop it; grounding or RAG reduces hallucinations but cannot eliminate them (not in docs). Models, image models included, make things up when information is missing.

Stage Any deception earns a page the lowest rater rating, and fake personas are a growing case: an author bio with an AI-generated headshot of a person who doesn't exist. Raters can't penalise a site, but their ratings become labels that improve the algorithms, so unchecked AI output harms indirectly. Docs The rater guidelines (September 2025) rate made-up author profiles Lowest; no single rating changes a page's ranking directly. Analysis Show only real authors with real photos and verifiable profiles, and check AI output before it goes live.

AI slop is scaled content abuse

Stage Pages once churned out with Perl or PHP scripts are now churned out with LLMs, and cheaper tokens mean more AI slop. Google counts it as scaled content abuse and goes to great lengths to remove it. Citing its rater guidelines: "it's not the tool that was used that is the problem, but rather how the tool was used and what for." Docs Google added this to its generative AI content guidance on 1 October 2026.

The closing slides

Slide Use AI to supercharge workflows, content, website and customer management, but responsibly: AI hallucinates, so don't add to the sea of AI slop.

"AI features on Google Search use exactly the same processes as traditional results."Closing slide: "AI on Google is just SEO", no new acronym needed

Stage AI Overviews and AI Mode sit on 25 to 30 years of Search infrastructure, with very few processes of their own. Gary Illyes: "To me, that means that SEO is not dead." Search changed every year, from the Florida update in 2003 to the Knowledge Graph (not in docs), each time with new opportunities; shiny things such as guessing fan-out queries distract from helpful content. Web Guide groups results into categories with summaries. From the community talks he judged that most SEOs have a very good handle on Search. Docs A Search Labs experiment since July 2025, using a custom Gemini and query fan-out.

Day 3 action checklist

Checks that turn Day 3 into work, grouped for the content team, the development team and whoever reports on Search.
Content team
  • Pages use the words and spellings the audience types; no synonym lists; variants checked in Search first
  • Reviews and AI-assisted articles add own tests, photos and expertise beyond what an LLM would answer
  • Product Q&As answer real customer questions from support tickets, reviews and sales chats
  • Query groups are read as intent themes: one page per intent, not one per phrasing
  • Company details, policies, reviews and contact are indexable pages; "[brand] legit" queries are tracked
  • Only real authors with real photos and verifiable profiles; no invented personas or AI headshots
Development team
  • Each page has one main language and a clear target country, with hreflang between the versions
  • The back button works; Googlebot sees what users see; no doorway pages; the CMS is patched
  • Articles allow max-image-preview:large with an image at least 1,200 px wide; WebSite markup names the site
  • Feed and Product markup carry identical values; Storebot-Google is not blocked on shop paths
  • Critical content, links and meta tags are in the HTML the server sends
  • robots.txt changes ship a day early; redirects stay a year; urgent removals use the Removals tool
Measurement and reporting
  • The AI report is read as impressions; visits, conversions and revenue come from your own analytics
  • Social accounts are platform properties; multimodal traffic is judged by page, country and device
  • Large sites use the BigQuery bulk export; the branded split is spot-checked before LLM analysis
  • After a core update: compare a full week after rollout with the week before, then review the whole site
